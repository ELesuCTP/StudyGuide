# Review of the supplied Chapter 28 examples

## What the examples establish
The eight supplied posters consistently use a playful hand-drawn nursing message-board style: bold outlined display titles, pastel rounded panels, yellow emphasis, pink checkmarks, topic-specific cartoons, and compact review boxes. Panels are modular but the page does not read as a strict spreadsheet or uniform table. Panel size and combination respond to the content.

The set demonstrates useful panel roles: overview/disorder list; definition or diagnostic criteria; clinical course and population notes; factors, etiology, or epidemiology; assessment and diagnostic testing; treatment and named medications; patient education and nursing care; safety; quick memory.

## Additional panel types recommended
The examples include safety and medication cautions, but do not give complications a consistent, dedicated visual treatment. Add a **Complication Watch** panel when the assigned source identifies meaningful complications or functional consequences. Give it a distinct warning color/icon and a clear sequence: complication/consequence, what the nurse may observe, then a safety or response point. Do not add a complication just to fill space.

Optional variants:
- **Red Flag / Act Now:** urgent cues and the immediate escalation or safety response stated in the source.
- **Medication Watch:** named drug/class, intended effect, high-value adverse effects or interactions, and monitoring/teaching.
- **Compare at a Glance:** distinguishing cues for two conditions/concepts, without turning the whole page into a grid.
- **Clinical Cue → Nursing Move:** a short scenario, cue, and source-supported first action.

These are reusable patterns, not mandatory boxes on every page. Keep the composition loose and include only panels that fit the source.

## Source accuracy note
The Chapter 28 images are visual examples only. Their content must not be treated as a source for another chapter. For example, older-adult falls and cognitive/mood concerns in the insomnia example, sleepwalking injury precautions, and sedative safety notes belong in a guide only when the assigned source supports them.
