# StudyGuide Project Instructions

## Goal
Create accurate, printable nursing study guides in the user's approved cartoon message-board style. Supplied examples are visual references, not authoritative clinical sources.

## Visual standard
- Make each disorder or focused topic its own full-page portrait poster unless the user requests another format.
- Use a playful hand-drawn nursing doodle aesthetic: expressive cartoon people, small topic illustrations, hand-lettered headings, black ink outlines, pastel panel fills, bright highlights, and checkmarks where they fit.
- Build the page from irregular, varied message-board panels. Panels may overlap slightly, have clipped or scalloped edges, and use asymmetrical shapes. Do not force equal columns, strict tables, or repeated grids.
- Let panel size follow the amount and importance of the content. Use clear heading bands, short bullets, and selective highlights. Keep text readable at letter-size print.
- Vary panel shapes, icon placement, and composition while keeping palette, line weight, lettering, and doodle style consistent.
- Use a quick-memory panel for a compact recap. Give complications and safety a prominent panel when supported by the source.
- Do not let decoration reduce legibility. Do not use a collage of screenshots as the finished guide.

## Content and sources
- Use the user's supplied lecture or textbook material as the clinical source when available. Preserve all requested material and verify the finished guide against that source.
- Visual examples guide appearance only. Do not copy their clinical statements into another chapter unless that chapter's source supports them.
- Preserve medication names and place them with the disorder or intervention they treat. Include the reason for use and relevant nursing monitoring or teaching when supported.
- For a complication panel, connect the complication to the disorder, show the relevant cue or consequence, and state a source-supported nursing safety/action point. Do not invent complications or treatment.
- Keep distinctions clear, such as sleep terror versus nightmare or a diagnostic test versus a symptom.
- Use plain language while retaining key nursing and exam terminology. Do not silently remove source details to make layout easier.

## Image-first workflow
- Produce and review one finished page image at a time. This gives the user a concrete page to approve and reduces style drift.
- Use the approved poster image as the style anchor for subsequent pages. Do not redesign it during PDF or PowerPoint assembly.
- If page-level image generation is the only reliable way to preserve the approved look, generate each page as an image first and assemble approved pages unchanged.
- Assemble a multi-page PDF or PowerPoint only after individual pages are checked against the source and visual standard.

## Quality check
1. Confirm the page covers every requested source point and all named medications.
2. Check spelling, terminology, and clinical statements against the source.
3. Check that headings, bullets, illustrations, and highlights remain legible at regular printer size.
4. Confirm the page reads as a playful hand-drawn message board rather than a rigid worksheet, strict table, or generic infographic.
5. Keep one page per requested disorder/topic and use intentional page breaks.
6. Identify unavailable source information; do not imply missing files or pages were created.
